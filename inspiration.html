Colour inspiration

Find a direction before you choose a shade

Explore original mood palettes and practical ways to combine colour. Use these ideas as a starting point, then visit us in Matara to compare real paint samples and available manufacturer shades.

Start with the feeling

Colour works best when it begins with the space

Instead of starting with one exact shade, think about how you want the room to feel: calm, warm, fresh, bright or dramatic. From there, build a small palette with a main colour, a supporting tone and one controlled accent.

The palettes below are visual mood references created for The Colour Alchemy. They are not manufacturer shade names or guaranteed paint matches.

Build your palette

A simple three-part approach

01

Main colour

Choose the colour that will cover the largest area. It should work with the room's natural light, flooring and fixed finishes.

02

Supporting tone

Add a related lighter, darker or warmer tone for another wall, joinery or adjoining space so the scheme feels connected.

03

Controlled accent

Use the strongest colour in a smaller area — a feature, door, furniture detail or selected architectural element.

Before you decide

Colour is only part of the result

Light

A colour can look different near windows, in shaded areas and under warm or cool artificial lighting.

Finish

Matt, low-sheen and gloss finishes reflect light differently, which changes the way a colour reads across a surface.

Surroundings

Flooring, curtains, furniture, roof colours and other fixed materials should be considered before finalising the palette.
